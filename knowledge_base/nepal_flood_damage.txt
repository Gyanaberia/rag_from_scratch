Casualties
Surveillance footage showing floodwater destroying the backside of Gyirong Port, looking upstream along the Trishuli River.
As of 5 September, at least 1,357 people died,[3] 6,827 were injured,[3] and 5,326 were missing in Nepal.[3] Among the missing were 590 foreign nationals from 39 countries.[64] According to the Ministry of Health on 1 September, just 4% of the roughly 900 bodies found so far had been identified.[65] The NDRRMA said on 2 September that nearly 1.6 million people had been affected by the floods.[66]
As of 6 September, 43 people died and 519 were missing in Tibet.[4] Among the missing in Tibet include 261 foreign nationals from 23 countries.[4] This includes 104 Nepali nationals.[67]
Among those missing as of 2 September are over 900 workers on hydropower projects in Nepal.[68] Of the total number missing, it is believed that there are 288 Indian nationals, 127 Nepali tourists, 75 Americans,[69] 53 Ukrainians, 51 Malaysians, and 35 Australians.[70] On 26 August, it was reported that there were 33 UK nationals missing.[71][72] The Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs reported nearly 100 Chinese citizens in Nepal missing.[38][73] Australia's foreign minister said on 27 August that at least 34 of the missing were pilgrims.[38][74] A group of 77 Isha Foundation pilgrims from 19 different nationalities traveling to Tibet were at Gyirong Port during the flash flood.[75] The Canadian Government reported on 29 August that 32 citizens are missing.[76] The South Korean Foreign Ministry reported 9 citizens working at a hydropower plant construction site went missing.[77] The Latvian Foreign Ministry reported 33 Latvians missing on 29 August.[78][79]
At least 147 government officials, including eight local elected representatives, 32 local government employees, 11 teachers,[80] 25 police officers,[3] 12 Armed Police Force personnel,[3] 45 Nepali Army personnel,[3] 15 customs office employees, and 4 immigration office employees were reported missing.[81] Around 622 students were also unaccounted for.[82] An estimated 300 trade and transportation workers were reported missing.[83] Over 900 people involved in 12 hydropower projects were reported missing, with 500 believed to be trapped in tunnels.[84][68] At the Upper Trishuli 1 Hydroelectric Project, at least 576 workers were reported missing, with around 300 trapped inside the project's tunnel.[85] On September 7, the Nepal Army said it believed that there were 121 people trapped inside affected hydropower tunnels.[86]
The International Federation of Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies estimated that 93,000 people were impacted by the floods[87] and the United Nations estimated that 65,000 people were in need of emergency water, sanitation and hygiene materials.[85] UN spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric stated that an estimated 2,600 children younger than 5 required treatment for severe wasting and 10,500 pregnant and breastfeeding women required nutrition assistance.[85] UNICEF estimated that 17,000 children were impacted by the floods.[88] UN Women estimated that 157,050 women and girls were impacted by the floods and 43,000 women and girls were in need of humanitarian aid.[89]
The flood carried the bodies of some victims 240 km (150 mi) away,[90] with bodies recovered as far downstream as Nawalparasi District in Nepal[91] and Kushinagar and Maharajganj districts in Uttar Pradesh, India.[92]

Damage
Satellite imagery before and after the flooding. Trishuli (Bhotekoshi) is left, Lhende Khola at the top, Chhochen Khola top right, probable glacier scarp centre right, Langtang Lirung bottom right.
It was estimated that the resulting debris traveled at a speed of around 50 m/s (180 km/h; 110 mph),[112] while analysis of satellite images suggests that discolorations caused by the flood reached an elevation of 800 m (2,600 ft) in a valley some 7 km (4.3 mi) from the origin.[113] Following the flood, more than 1.5 m (4 ft 11 in) of silt was deposited in the main disaster zone.[114] The United Nations Development Programme estimated that the flooding generated 2.2 million tonnes of debris, including 556,200 tonnes of building debris.[115]

Floodwaters reached settlements as high as 80 metres (270 ft) above the river level.[116]

Nepal
In Nepal, residential areas and infrastructure, including roads, sustained extensive damage.[117][118] The districts of Rasuwa, Nuwakot, Dhading, Gorkha, and Chitwan were impacted by the flooding.[119] Around 8,317 houses were destroyed,[120] along with 48 government buildings and seven health facilities.[121] In Nuwakot, floodwater entered areas including Timure, Dhunche, Mailung, Syafrubesi, Betrawati, Trishuli, and Devighat.[116][118][122] A preliminary estimate by the Nepal government reported that the floods caused over रू 400 billion (US$2.64 billion) in damages.[5] Finance minister Swarnim Wagle estimated that preliminary rebuilding costs could reach रू 900 billion (US$6 billion).[123]

The Roads Department estimated रू 30 billion in road and bridge infrastructure had been damaged,[124] impacting 68 suspension bridges, 33 driveable bridges, and 55 km (34 mi) of road.[121] The government deemed 33 suspension bridges damaged beyond repair.[125] A road connecting Syafrubesi and Rasuwagadhi, part of the Trishuli Corridor (NH42), which was under reconstruction after damage from flooding in 2025, was further damaged.[126] A bridge connected to Trishuli Bazar was swept away.[127] A section of the Prithvi Highway from Baireni to Mugling was blocked until 28 August,[128] while another section of the highway between Mugling and Kathmandu was closed due to erosion from the Trishuli River.[129]

Around 1,806 ha (4,460 acres) of agricultural lands were damaged in the floods,[130] while a large number of livestock were killed.[131] Four flood monitoring stations were destroyed.[36][132] Around 1,200 people from at least three villages were displaced.[38] The rural municipality offices in Gosaikunda Rural Municipality and Uttargaya Rural Municipality were washed away.[133]

At least 23 schools were damaged in Rasuwa, Nuwakot and Dhading districts, impacting over 7,000 students.[134] The Rasuwagadhi customs office was destroyed,[116] while more than 300 vehicles queuing up at the site were swept away,[135] along with around 1,000 electric vehicles and containers with spare parts being exported into Nepal.[136] An estimated 100 to 200 loaded shipping containers were in the customs yard at the time of the flood and are assumed missing.[83] Nepal Telecom said 120 of its towers had been affected, while Ncell said 78 towers had been affected[121] with losses of more than रू 90 million.[137]

Power infrastructure
Damage to electricity generation, transmission, and distribution infrastructure disrupted supplies in affected areas. The Nepal Electricity Authority (NEA) said that 13 hydropower projects had been damaged, leaving hundreds of billions of rupees in damage.[138] These included Rasuwagadhi, Chilime, Trishuli 3A, the Trishuli 3B Hub 220 kV substation, Trishuli Hydropower Station, and Devighat Hydropower Station.[139] The NEA reported that damage to energy infrastructure had removed an estimated 431 megawatts of electricity off of the grid and that projects under construction with a total capacity of 470 megawatts were damaged.[140] An estimated रू 100 billion in damage to hydroelectric projects was reported.[123]

Australian tunnel rescue expert Arnold Dix, who was asked by Nepalese authorities to assist with rescue efforts at Upper Trishuli 3A Hydropower Station, where 42 workers are reported missing, said that 27 power stations were "wiped out", with 12 "destroyed, [with] nothing left at the surface".[68]

China
In China, road access, communications, and electricity supply to the Gyirong Port area were cut off by the flood,[141][142] affecting a section of China National Highway 216.[143] Chinese rescue workers surveilled the area on 28 August, with one describing the scene as "nothing but ruins".[144] Twenty-seven buildings were destroyed in the area.[145] Around 80 trucks and 170 containers were stranded in the town of Gyirong after flooding destroyed about 5 km (3.1 mi) of road running from the town to Rasuwagadhi.[146][83]

Further disruptions occurred on 31 August, when Chinese authorities closed the Tatopani border crossing due to risks of landslides, leaving the Korala border crossing in Mustang District as the only open thoroughfare across the Nepal-China border.[147]
