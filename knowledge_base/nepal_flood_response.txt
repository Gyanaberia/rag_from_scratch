Response
Nepal
The Government of Nepal mobilised the Nepali Army, Armed Police Force, and Nepal Police, supported by helicopters and medical teams, to conduct search, rescue, and relief operations.[148][149] More than 21,465 security personnel were deployed to assist rescue efforts, 9,287 personnel from the army, 7,912 from the police, and 4,203 from the APF.[150] Bidur hosted the primary staging ground for the army's search and rescue operations.[151] The Council of Ministers declared the area a disaster-hit zone for three months under the Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Act, 2074 (2017).[152][153] The government also offered free medical treatment for those injured and financial assistance to families who lost members.[154][152] On 27 August, Prime Minister Balen Shah arrived in Nuwakot District to monitor the situation.[155] The Prime Minister's Natural Disaster Relief Fund was launched by the government, raising over रू 11.77 billion by 8 September.[156] A separate account for dollar donations raised at least US$6 million.[157] Shah and his cabinet also pledged to donate one month's salary to the fund.[158] The government also released रू 675 million to 15 local units in five affected districts from its Disaster Management Fund.[159] It also requested assistance from the UN Loss and Damage Fund.[160]

Nepali officials reported that over 13,583 people had been rescued,[3] including 324 foreign nationals.[64] Initial rescue efforts were hampered by heavy rain[161] and the absence of aviation assets by the Nepal Police.[162] As hospitals and morgues in affected areas reached full capacity, injured survivors and cadavers were transferred to other locations including Bhairahawa, Bharatpur, Butwal, Gorkha, Kathmandu, Nawalparasi, Palpa, and Pokhara.[91] At least three people died while receiving medical care.[66] As of 8 September, at least 1,357 bodies were recovered.[3] Due to a lack of cold storage, many unidentified bodies were temporarily buried until they could be identified,[163] with DNA testing and facial photographs used to identify bodies.[164][165] By 7 September, 1,044 bodies were buried and 1,181 bodies had DNA sampled.[166] Some family members of the victims practice criticised the practice, alleging that it ignored traditional Hindu funeral traditions.[165] Other families who had not found their deceased relatives resorted to using effigies made with kusha grass in their funeral rituals.[167]

Displaced persons camps were set up in Nuwakot and Dhading.[168] In Trishuli Bazar, residents reported looting in houses with very few police officers to maintain order.[169] Several days after the disaster, headteacher Rajendra Dawadi was praised for evacuating 900 children, from Tribhuvan Trishuli secondary school in Nuwakot District, ten minutes before the flash flood. He had received four warnings from different sources, including one from the town of Betrawati, directly upstream of the school.[170][171]

Following the flooding, the Nepal Roads Department began installing bailey bridges to replace collapsed bridges.[126] The Nepal Army delivered eight tonnes of relief supplies to Rasuwa by 27 August.[172] The government deployed over 65 mental health professionals in Rasuwa and Nuwakot, and launching a 24/7 mental health helpline.[173][174] By 3 September, over 1,239 people had received with psychological first aid, counseling, and mental health services.[173] It also sent 500 portable solar systems to Rasuwa as part of efforts to provide electricity to households,[175] and deployed a fuel tanker to Trishuli for supplying aircraft engaged in relief efforts.[176]

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs initially turned down offers from several countries to send their search-and-rescue teams to support relief operations, citing mismanagement and poor coordination from the joint rescue effort following the 2015 earthquake as the main reason for the decision.[177][178] However, the Nepalese government allowed for the entry of experts from India, China, Australia, and South Korea for help in tunnel rescues, DNA testing and forensic examinations.[179] It also requested heavy-lift cargo drones from international partners to deliver relief supplies to isolated mountain regions.[180]

In the early hours of 31 August, a controlled explosion was carried out in a tunnel at the Upper Trishuli 3A Hydropower Station, in an effort to locate 933 missing workers. The Nepali Army then dug a hole about 10 m (33 ft) deep, using two excavators, and began to clear water, mud and debris from the tunnel.[181] Two survivors were rescued from the tunnel on 4 September,[182] while a Chinese national was rescued from the tunnel the next day. Separately, a woman was rescued from a buried house in Betrawati on 5 September.[183] By 4 September, Nepal Telecom reported that 116 of its 120 impacted cellular towers had been restored and Ncell reported that 75 of its 78 impacted cellular towers had been restored.[121]

A national day of mourning for the victims of the disaster was held in Nepal on 7 September.[184]

China
CCP general secretary Xi Jinping called for continued efforts to locate missing people and improvements to disaster monitoring and warning systems.[185][186] The government of the Tibet Autonomous Region mobilised nearly 800 emergency workers and 150 vehicles as well as sniffer dogs and speed boats to help the rescue efforts.[187] Units of the People's Armed Police,[188] the People's Liberation Army and rescue teams from neighbouring Sichuan province were deployed to assist in rescue operations.[189][87] The National Fire and Rescue Administration also sent 681 personal and 13 rescue dogs, while the Ministry of Emergency Management sent 60 personnel.[190] Two people were rescued at Gyirong Port. Access to the site was hampered by the accumulation of debris about 3 km (1.9 mi) upstream.[191] Chinese state media reported that 499 residents and 555 tourists had been evacuated from the area in Tibet.[87]

Premier Li Qiang arrived in Gyirong on 26 August.[192][38] The China Meteorological Administration set up portable automatic weather stations in Gyirong Port.[193] A 4G/5G base station was installed in Gyirong County to remediate disrupted communication infrastructure.[194] The China National Space Administration stated that it coordinated and mobilised satellite resources to perform emergency imaging of the impacted area.[195] Rescue operator China Anneng deployed 158 personnel and 33 heavy equipment to repair roads.[143] Road repair efforts were hampered by river currents flowing up ​to six times faster than normal river speeds.[143] A makeshift road connecting to Gyirong Port was completed by 2 September.[64] Chinese authorities arranged for the repatriation of least 114 Nepalis who were stranded in China through the Tatopani border crossing,[196] while 576 others were repatriated through the Kodari crossing.[197]

On the morning of 30 August, the Government of China delivered 50 tonnes of emergency relief supplies to Kathmandu via two Xi'an Y-20 transport aircraft. The supplies were handed over by Chinese ambassador Zhang Maoming to Nepalese science minister Mahabir Pun.[198] Later that day, Ambassador Zhang Maoming delivered US$2 million in emergency flood-relief assistance from the Government of China to Nepalese Finance Minister Swarnim Wagle. He also handed over an additional US$200,000 contributed by the Red Cross Society of China to Nepal's Prime Minister Disaster Relief Fund.[199] On 1 September, a second batch of relief supplies was delivered to Nepal from China, including drones, DNA testing equipment, and water purification equipment.[200] The government also deployed a team of tunnel experts to aid rescue efforts at hydropower station tunnels and a team of forensics experts to Nepal.[199][201] The government sent two bailey bridges to Nepal.[124]

Censorship and state media coverage
Further information: Censorship in China and Public opinion guidance
Coverage of the disaster within China ranged from outright censorship to portrays in line with official "public opinion guidance."[202] Videos of the disaster taken by locals in Tibet and eyewitness accounts were censored on Chinese social media platforms.[203][204][205] Foreign journalists are not permitted to enter the region except on government-supervised trips, and Tibetans can face punishment for speaking to them.[203][206] Smaller Chinese state-media outlets initially published interviews with witnesses, but the articles were removed within hours.[207] The Ministry of Public Security said at least 10 people were penalised for sharing misleading or disruptive posts about the disaster.[208][209][210][211] China tightly controls information in Tibet, which it annexed in 1951, where protests over independence are suppressed. Chinese state media carried several broadcasts about the disaster and sent journalists to the affected area, although reports focused mainly on rescue efforts rather than the extent of the devastation.[206] The journalists as well as social media influencers were warned against reporting casualties different from officially announced figures.[204] A BBC headline claiming that coverage of the disaster was fully censored in China was changed to "heavily censored" after backlash from Chinese netizens.[19][205] Several foreign journalists said that Chinese authorities denied their requests to visit the affected areas citing difficult terrain and the risk of more floods.[207]

Restrictions on independent reporting made the extent of the damage on the Tibetan side difficult to verify.[206][33] The International Campaign for Tibet claimed that the casualties in Tibet were higher than what China reported, with up to 300 homes destroyed.[208] The deputy Asia director of Human Rights Watch said that Chinese state media journalists were told not to interview survivors or members of their family.[204] In response to accusations of censorship and underreporting casualties, Guo Jiakun, spokesperson of China's foreign ministry, objected to using the disaster for "smear campaigns and hype".[208] On 6 September, Chinese authorities hosted a group of foreign journalists on a visit to the rescue area which had been overrun by mud. Recovered bodies were still in the process of being identified.[111]

International organisations
